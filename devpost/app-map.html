Takeable — app map

A one-page guide to where things live. Snapshot of commit b5a3ecd; the code may have moved on since.

What it does

Takeable asks NYU's public class search for a term's online and blended undergraduate sections. It then judges each section for one student's situation and labels it Takeable, Check or Not takeable, with reasons. It weighs three things:

  • Whether the class meets inside the student's class hours, on their own clock.
  • Whether their home school gives credit. Stern's published rules are built in.
  • Whether NYU's section note sets a restriction.

Server

server.mjs serves the page and two routes. lib/nyu.mjs talks to NYU; lib/cache.mjs keeps a 6-hour cache and the offline snapshot in data/snapshots/.

Judging

Pure functions in public/core/: time zones (time.js), notes (notes.js), credit rules (schools.js), and the verdict (verdict.js). The tests in tests/ exercise these directly.

Page

public/app.js holds the state, draws the board, cards, 24-hour bars, detail panel and shortlist, and saves choices in local storage. Styling is in public/styles.css.

One action, end to end: switching the city

When you change “I'll be in Beijing” to London, every verdict and every 24-hour bar updates without asking NYU again:

Reference route (three stops; not toured together)

  1. public/app.js → onSituationChange. It reads the new city's time zone, saves the situation to local storage, and calls render(). Nothing is fetched again, because judging happens in the page.
  2. public/core/verdict.js → judge and timeReason. It collects the time, credit and note reasons, then sorts them fail → check → pass. Any fail makes the section Not takeable; any check makes it Check.
  3. public/core/time.js → localSchedule and fitsWindow. It reads the New York meeting time on the real session date, converts it to the student's zone, and tests it against their class hours. Back in app.js, dialHtml draws the same result as the coloured block on the 24-hour bar.

A practice to reuse: write the check before the change

What happened. Each acceptance criterion under prd.md › Takeability Verdict became a test in tests/verdict.test.mjs during build slice 2. Two examples: TECH-UB 25 is Not takeable from Beijing, and ECON1-UC 301 gets no Stern credit. Later, the final review asked for a full visual redesign, and commit b5a3ecd rewrote the rendering in public/app.js and nearly all of styles.css (656 lines added, 352 removed).

The evidence. npm test passed 26 of 26 before and after the redesign. So the large change to how verdicts look provably didn't change what they say.

Reuse it. When you ask an agent for a big change, first pin the behaviour you care about as observable checks, even three or four. Then the agent can move fast, and you can tell in seconds whether it broke something, instead of re-reading the whole diff.
